In 2026, two other student leaders and I started rebuilding our school's **FIRST Robotics Competition** (FRC) team for the **2027 season**. The game is **BIOCORE**. We started with no robot and no budget.

## The problem

Tri-County had an FRC team years ago that went dormant in 2019. We aren't picking up where it left off. We're registering as a **brand-new rookie team**, so its history isn't ours to claim. Everything on this page is what we've built since starting over.

FRC is a lot bigger than VEX, which I've done since 2021. The robots are full size, the parts are real metal, and registering alone costs real money. So the first problem wasn't the robot. It was:

- **Money:** no budget, and no sponsors yet.
- **People:** new students who have never touched an FRC part.
- **Credibility:** a company deciding whether to fund us needs to see we're real.

## The solution

- **Cold-email local companies** to sponsor a rookie team.
- **Build a team website** so those companies can see who we are.
- **Build a warm-up robot** so the team learns FRC parts and how to work together before the real season.
- **Set team rules early**, so decisions get made on evidence instead of on who's loudest.

### The LeBron bot

Before the real season we needed something to build. We built the **LeBron bot**: a **basketball shooter on a tank chassis**. It's a goofy name for a practice run at an FRC-style drivetrain, wiring and programming.

### Two team rules

We set two rules early:

- **Ideas go on the board with no names.** Nobody's idea wins because it's theirs, and nobody's gets ignored because they're new. We judge the sketch, not the person.
- **If it can be tested in under 10 minutes, we test it.** We don't debate something we could just try. Build the quick version, run it, and let the result settle it.

### Why FRC fits a vocational school

At a lot of schools, FRC is the only place students touch a mill or crimp a wire. At Tri-County, students already learn those skills in their **shop programs**. FRC gives them a reason to use all of it on one machine: design, machining, wiring and programming, done by students training to do this for a living.
