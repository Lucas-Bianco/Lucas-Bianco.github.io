Most of what I make starts as a problem on my desk: something that needs a holder, a mount, a bin or a replacement part. I design it in SolidWorks, print it, see what's wrong, and print it again. This page is about the setup and the process, and where I want to take it next.

## The printers

I run two Bambu Lab printers: an X2D and an A1. Both have an AMS, the automatic material system that feeds filament from several spools, so a print can switch colors or materials partway through without me standing there swapping spools.

Having two machines changes how I work. One can run a long print while the other handles quick test pieces. When I'm dialing in a fit, that's the difference between one test an hour and several.

## Designing in SolidWorks

SolidWorks is where I do almost all of my modeling, and I hold the CSWA and CSWP certifications for it. I try to build models the way a shop would want them: fully defined sketches, dimensions that mean something, and features I can change later without the model falling apart.

That last part matters more for printing than people think. A print that's 0.2 mm too tight is a quick dimension change in a well-built model, and a rebuild from scratch in a messy one.

## Fit testing on SKÅDIS and Gridfinity

The hard part of printing isn't getting a part to come out. It's getting it to fit something else. Every printer and filament prints a little differently, so a part that's perfect on paper can be too tight to slide in or loose enough to rattle.

I use two systems as my fit references:

- **SKÅDIS** is IKEA's pegboard. The holes are a fixed size and spacing, so a hook or holder either clicks in and holds or it doesn't. It's a fast, honest test.
- **Gridfinity** is an open storage system built on a 42 mm grid. Bins and baseplates from different designers are all supposed to fit together, so if my bin drops into a baseplate cleanly, my dimensions and my printer are both behaving.

Because both are standards, they take the guessing out. I'm not checking a part against another part I also printed. I'm checking it against a known size.

## MakerWorld plans

MakerWorld is Bambu's site for sharing printable models. I'm planning to publish sets of models there instead of one-off files: groups of parts that work together, like a family of SKÅDIS holders or Gridfinity bins for specific tools.

I have three reasons. Points on MakerWorld can go back into filament and parts. A public profile is a portfolio that shows real designs other people actually print. And it's a community: people print your stuff, find the problems you missed, and tell you.
