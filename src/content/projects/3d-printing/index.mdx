## The problem

Getting a part to come out of a printer is the easy part. Getting it to **fit something else** is hard.

Every printer and every filament prints a little differently. A part that's perfect in CAD can come out too tight to slide in, or loose enough to rattle. If you only check a printed part against another printed part, you never know which one is off.

## The solution

A setup built around three things:

- **Two printers**, so long prints and quick test pieces don't wait on each other.
- **Clean SolidWorks models**, so a fit fix is a dimension change, not a redesign.
- **Standard fit references**, SKÅDIS and Gridfinity, so I'm checking against a known size instead of guessing.

## How it works

### The printers

I run two **Bambu Lab** printers: an **X2D** and an **A1**. Both have an **AMS**, the automatic material system that feeds filament from several spools. A print can switch colors or materials partway through without me swapping spools by hand.

With two machines, one can run a long print while the other turns out small test pieces. That makes dialing in a fit much faster.

### Designing in SolidWorks

I do almost all of my modeling in SolidWorks, and I hold the **CSWA and CSWP** certifications. I build models the way a shop would want them: fully defined sketches, dimensions that mean something, and features I can change later without the model breaking.

That matters for printing. A part that's 0.2 mm too tight is a quick dimension edit in a well-built model, and a rebuild from scratch in a messy one.

### Standards as fit references

- **SKÅDIS** is IKEA's pegboard. The holes have a fixed size and spacing, so a hook or holder either clicks in and holds or it doesn't.
- **Gridfinity** is an open storage system built on a **42 mm grid**. Bins and baseplates from different designers are supposed to fit together, so if my bin drops into a baseplate cleanly, my dimensions and my printer are both behaving.

Because both are standards, they take the guessing out of the check.

## Testing

For a new fit, I print small **test coupons**: the same peg or edge at several clearances, then try each one on the real reference.

## What I learned

- **Check against a known size.** Testing a print against a standard tells you what's wrong. Testing it against another print only tells you they disagree.
- **Model for the second version.** The first print rarely fits, so the model has to be easy to change.

## What's next

**MakerWorld** is Bambu's site for sharing printable models. I'm planning to publish **sets** of models instead of one-off files: groups of parts that work together, like a family of SKÅDIS holders or Gridfinity bins for specific tools.

- Points on MakerWorld can go back into filament and parts.
- A public profile is a portfolio of real designs other people actually print.
- People who print your models find the problems you missed and tell you.

I also want to write down my tolerances for each printer and material so I stop re-testing the same fits.
